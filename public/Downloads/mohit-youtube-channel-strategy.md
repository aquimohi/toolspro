# YouTube Channel Strategy — Mohit (Delhi)

## The core insight

Your skill stack — GAS/web dev + QGIS/ArcGIS mapping + data scraping + Power BI/Excel + AI tools — isn't "random." Put together, it's a **data investigator** toolkit. Almost nobody in the Hindi/Hinglish tech-YouTube space combines maps + data + automation + real-world experiments with a personal face. Tech YouTubers do reviews/tutorials. Vloggers do vlogs. Almost nobody says "let me pull real data about a real Indian problem, map it, build a small tool around it, and show you what I found." That gap is your opening.

---

## 5 Channel Concepts

### 1. **"Data Detective Delhi"** — investigate real Indian/Delhi questions using data + maps
You pick a real, relatable question ("Which Delhi metro line actually saves you the most time?", "Where in India does it *actually* rain the most in monsoon?", "Which Noida sector has the most potholes complaints?"), scrape/collect real data, map it in QGIS, visualize it in Power BI, and narrate the investigation — ending with a tool/dashboard you built. Face-forward, detective/investigator framing.

### 2. **"Mohit Builds"** — weekend builder journal
Every weekend you build something (a Sheet-based tool, a website, a small automation, an AI-powered app) that solves a real problem for you or a friend, filmed as a build-log with your face reacting, explaining, debugging on camera.

### 3. **"₹0 Tech Jugaad"** — low-budget tech experiments and hacks
Comedy-adjacent tech experiments using cheap/old hardware (8GB RAM PC, old S10 phone) — "can an i3 PC run X," "I automated my job with Apps Script," DIY tech fixes. Broader appeal but closer to generic tech content — riskier for differentiation.

### 4. **"India Mapped"** — pure GIS storytelling channel
Deep-dive map-based documentaries about India using QGIS/ArcGIS (historical borders, migration, elections, geography). Strong unique-content potential but narrower audience and harder to sustain weekly without new data sources.

### 5. **"The Analyst's Life"** — personal-brand hybrid of career + data + AI + DIY
A broader "journey" channel where the throughline is *you*, a low-budget Delhi techie exploring data, AI, GIS and automation as a career/life skill, including career/salary/job-market content. Most "personal brand" potential but least focused niche.

## Strongest Concept: **Data Detective Delhi** (Concept 1, with Concept 2 folded in as a recurring segment)

**Why this wins over the others:**
- It's genuinely rare — GIS + data scraping + dashboards + Hindi delivery is a combination almost no one is doing on YouTube.
- It has a *built-in format* (investigate → collect data → map/visualize → reveal answer) that works for hundreds of episodes because India never runs out of local questions worth investigating.
- It uses **every** skill you listed (GAS, web tools, QGIS/ArcGIS, scraping, Power BI, Excel, AI tools) as different *stages of one story*, not a random skill dump.
- It's naturally face-forward and personality-driven (you're the "detective," the audience follows your reasoning) without needing fluent English or expensive production.
- It has a clear monetization and brand path: local businesses/govt-data-adjacent sponsors, freelance data/dashboard work, eventually paid tools or courses.
- "Mohit Builds" becomes a monthly/biweekly segment inside the same channel (not a separate channel) — so your existing GAS/web-building skill has a home too, without splitting your identity.

## Channel Category / Niche
**Hindi/Hinglish data-investigation and mapping channel** — real Indian questions answered with real data, maps, and small tools, hosted by a relatable, low-budget, self-taught tech creator.

## Target Audience
- Curious young Indians (18–30) who like "did-you-know" and Indian-city/geography content but are tired of generic facts videos.
- Students/early professionals interested in data analysis, GIS, or tech careers, especially those who can't afford paid courses.
- People who enjoy mystery/investigation-style storytelling (true-crime-adjacent audience) applied to everyday Indian topics instead of crime.
- Delhi/NCR locals who'll specifically enjoy city-focused episodes.

## Your USP
"**Ek Delhi ka ladka jo kisi bhi sawaal ka jawaab data aur naqshe (maps) se dhoondta hai** — using tools most people don't know exist, on a budget most people can actually afford." You're not the polished, expensive-studio data-analyst; you're the scrappy, self-taught, camera-in-your-room investigator who *shows the process*, mistakes included.

## What videos you SHOULD make
- "Investigation" episodes: real question → data collection → map/dashboard → answer (your core format).
- "Build" episodes: turning an investigation's output into a small tool/dashboard/website.
- Myth-busting with data ("Kya Delhi sach me India ka sabse pollution wala shehar hai — poora data dekhte hain").
- Local/hyperlocal deep-dives (your own colony, metro line, market, college) — cheap to produce, highly relatable.
- "I tried to answer this with AI vs manually" comparison episodes — shows your AI fluency without becoming an "AI tutorial" channel.
- Viewer-submitted questions ("aap logo ne poocha, maine data se dhoonda").

## What videos you should NOT make
- Generic "top 10 AI tools" or "ChatGPT tutorial" videos — oversaturated, no personality edge.
- Generic tech news/unboxing — you don't have the budget to compete and it doesn't use your unique skills.
- Pure vlogging with no data/investigation hook — dilutes the identity you're building.
- Clickbait "shocking truth" videos not actually backed by real data — breaks trust, which is your core asset.
- Anything requiring expensive gear/travel you can't sustain weekly.

## How to use your face and personality
Be the on-camera narrator throughout — not just intro/outro. Show your actual screen work (QGIS, spreadsheets, code) intercut with your face reacting, explaining, getting stuck, and figuring it out. Your imperfect English is fine — Hinglish, spoken naturally, is *more* trustworthy for this audience than polished English. Let curiosity and honest confusion be your on-camera personality; you're not performing expertise, you're performing the process of finding out.

## How to use AI in the content
Use AI tools **visibly, as part of the investigation** — e.g., using an AI tool to clean scraped data, generate a first-draft script structure, or build a quick prototype site — but always show your own judgment overriding or correcting the AI. This keeps AI as a supporting character in your story, not the whole show, which also protects you from the crowded "AI tutorial" space.

## How to use your skills without looking random
Frame every skill as one *stage* of the same repeatable pipeline: **Question → Scrape/collect data → Clean (Excel/Sheets) → Map (QGIS/ArcGIS) or visualize (Power BI) → Build a small tool (GAS/web) → Reveal answer on camera.** Viewers learn to expect this pipeline every episode, so GIS, coding, data, and AI all feel like one coherent "how Mohit solves things," not five separate topics.

## Branding Direction
**Channel name options:**
- **"Data Se Dhoondo"** (Find it with data) — punchy, explains the format instantly.
- **"Mohit Maps It"** — simple, ties to your name, memorable, works for English titles too.
- **"Naqsha Detective"** (Map Detective) — leans into the investigation/mystery hook.
- **"The Data Detective — Mohit"**

**Recommended: "Data Se Dhoondo"** with your face/name as the visible host brand (so it can grow into "Mohit" as personal brand later, the way channel and creator names often merge over time).

**Tagline:** *"Har sawaal ka jawaab, data aur naqshe se."* (Every question, answered with data and maps.)

## Thumbnail Style
Your face (curious/surprised expression) on one side, a bold India/city map or chart snippet on the other, with a short punchy Hindi/Hinglish question as text (3–5 words max). Consistent color accent (e.g., one signature color like electric blue or saffron) across every thumbnail so the channel is recognizable at a glance.

## Video Format
8–14 minute long-form "investigation" episodes, mid-week or weekend upload, shot in your room with the camera/tripod setup you already have. Screen-recording + face-cam intercut, natural spoken Hinglish, no heavy scripting — light structure, real reactions.

## Repeatable Video Structure
1. **Hook (0:00–0:30):** State the question in an intriguing way, show a glimpse of the final map/answer.
2. **Why this question (0:30–1:30):** Personal or relatable reason you're investigating it.
3. **Data hunt (1:30–4:00):** Where/how you're getting the data — scraping, public datasets, surveys.
4. **Build/Map/Analyze (4:00–8:00):** Screen-recorded process — QGIS, Power BI, Sheets, AI tools — with face reactions.
5. **The reveal (8:00–10:00):** The answer, visualized clearly.
6. **Reflection/CTA (10:00–end):** What surprised you, ask viewers for the next question to investigate.

## Realistic 6-Month Strategy
- **Month 1:** Rebrand existing channel or clearly relaunch; publish 3–4 Phase 1 (hyperlocal) videos to nail the format cheaply. Focus entirely on consistency and clarity, not virality.
- **Month 2:** Continue Phase 1–2, start clipping Shorts from every long video, engage every comment personally.
- **Month 3:** Introduce first "Build" episode; start cross-posting Shorts to Instagram/YouTube both.
- **Month 4:** Move into Phase 2/3 topics with broader (national) appeal; ask viewers for question submissions.
- **Month 5:** Release first public dashboard/tool tied to a video — gives viewers something tangible and shareable.
- **Month 6:** Review analytics — double down on whichever sub-format (hyperlocal vs national vs build) performed best; plan Phase 4–6 accordingly.

## Growing Without Spending Money
- Use free/public datasets (data.gov.in, OpenStreetMap, Census data, RTI-sourced data) — no cost.
- Cross-post every Short to Instagram Reels/YouTube Shorts for double distribution.
- Collaborate with your 2–3 friends as recurring "field reporters" for local data collection — free manpower, adds personality variety.
- Engage in relevant subreddits/Twitter data-viz communities by sharing your maps/dashboards (not spamming links) — this audience shares good data visuals organically.
- Reuse your D5300 + existing room setup; the differentiator here is the *idea*, not production value.

## Monetization Path
- YouTube ad revenue once eligible (secondary income at first, given a data/analysis niche has lower CPM volume but higher-value audience).
- Freelance/consulting: dashboards, scraping, or GIS work for small businesses who see your videos as a portfolio.
- Paid tools: release a simple paid or freemium version of a dashboard/tool you build on-camera.
- Local sponsorships: businesses in the areas you map (real estate, delivery, local government-adjacent orgs) — your channel becomes a credible, data-literate local voice.
- Eventually, a small paid course/community for people who want to learn "the pipeline" (scraping → mapping → building) you demonstrate weekly.

## Turning It Into a Personal Brand
Once the channel proves the format, your personal brand becomes "the person who explains India through data and maps" — this can extend into a personal newsletter, a small SaaS tool (e.g., a public dashboard product), consulting/freelance data work under your own name, and eventually speaking/creator collaborations in the Indian data/tech education space. The channel is the top of funnel; the tools and freelance work are the business underneath it.
